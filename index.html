01 / RESEARCH OVERVIEW

From a CV to an actionable career path.

Students may struggle to translate coursework and personal projects into job requirements. This project proposes a transparent recommendation workflow that can be tested against a simple baseline.

◎

The problem

Job descriptions use varied language, while students may not know which of their skills are relevant or which gaps matter most.

⌁

The proposed solution

Extract skills from a student profile, compare them with vacancy requirements, rank opportunities, and explain each match.

↗

Practical value

Offer understandable recommendations and a shortlist of skills a student could develop before applying.

CENTRAL RESEARCH QUESTION

Can a transparent skills-matching approach produce more useful job recommendations than basic keyword matching?

?

02 / METHODOLOGY

Test the idea, not just the interface.

The planned study compares recommendation methods on a small, documented dataset. The final paper should report the actual dataset, procedure, limitations, and observed results.

01

Build a dataset

Create or collect a permitted set of anonymised student skill profiles and entry-level vacancy descriptions. Record the source and consent/privacy basis.

02

Normalize skills

Standardize equivalent terms such as “JS” and “JavaScript”, while retaining the original text for traceability.

03

Compare methods

Use keyword overlap as a baseline, a weighted skills score, and—if feasible—a semantic similarity method.

04

Evaluate and reflect

Measure ranking quality with suitable metrics and conduct a small usability review. Do not report results until tests are complete.

Precision@KHow many top recommendations are relevant?
Recall@KHow many relevant options appear in the top K?
Usability feedbackCan users understand the match explanation?
